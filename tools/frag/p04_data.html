04Data

Two sources feed every model: real pedestrians (ETH and Hotel, hand-annotated video from Zurich) and a social-force crowd simulator we wrote (foresight/domains/crowd.py). Real data answers "are the ranges honest for real people in a place the model never saw"; the simulator answers "what does a robot gain", because real recordings have no robot and nobody in them can react to one. This section says where each comes from, what exactly is simulated, how every characteristic of a person and their surroundings becomes a number, what each source produced, and which data problems were fixed. Facts cite file: function; tables marked "generated" are recomputed from the data files each time the page is built (tools/build_internal_ref.py, gen_data_*). The layer view of the same code is in S0, S1 and S2.

Real data: ETH and Hotel

Source
ETH Walking Pedestrians (Pellegrini, Ess, Schindler, van Gool, ICCV 2009; docs/REFERENCES.md [5]), the files as copied by OpenTraj (Amirian et al., ACCV 2020, [7]): datasets/ETH/seq_eth/obsmat.txt and seq_hotel/obsmat.txt, fetched by the curl lines in README.md ("How to run"). Raw files are kept in data/raw/<scene>/ (obsmat.txt, H.txt, info.txt) and not redistributed. UCY (Zara, Univ; Lerner et al. 2007) is not used.
Sensor and annotation
Overhead video, annotated by hand. data/raw/eth/info.txt: shot from the top of the ETH main building, Zurich, by Pellegrini and Ess in 2009, annotated at 2.5 fps (0.4 s). data/raw/hotel/info.txt: shot from the 4th floor of a hotel on Bahnhofstrasse, Zurich, at 25 fps, annotated at 2.5 fps. obsmat.txt holds world coordinates in metres and annotated velocities in m/s (columns frame, id, x, z, y, vx, vz, vy; z unused). H.txt is the image-to-world homography; we do not use it.
Conversion
foresight/domains/eth_ucy.py: convert(raw_dir, out_dir, window=60.0): x = column 2, y = column 4, vx = column 5, vy = column 7 (velocities kept as annotated, not recomputed); frame step = median frame difference (ETH 6 frames, Hotel 10 frames at 25 fps; 0.4 s in both); the recording is cut into 60 s windows, each an episode (150 steps); agent_id = the annotation id, so a person crossing a window edge appears in two episodes and their history restarts; kind person, radius 0.25 m (our choice), is_ego 0, regime = scene name; empty obstacles.csv; meta.bounds = None on purpose (B9: the bounding box of all positions is the camera's field of view, computed from future positions, and fed the wall features); draw_bounds for figures only.
Resampling and noise
None. The 0.4 s grid is kept, so horizons snap to it: 1.2, 2.0 and 4.8 s (D-1; 4.8 s only because published work reports it). Feature rows use sensor noise 0 (rows_f4_noise0.0_…), but the forward Kalman filter still runs, with q 0.1, r 0.1 tuned on the other scene (data/eth/kalman.json: tuned_on data/hotel; data/hotel/kalman.json: data/eth; D-26).
Splits
Never within a scene for claim A: context and training rows from one scene, test rows from the other, both directions (tools/claim_a.py, tools/intent_layer.py). Live variants Setting A: Hotel rows as bank, the first 6 ETH episodes as the live stream (tools/live_variants.py).
What it contains
Positions and velocities of everyone annotated in view, every 0.4 s. ETH 8,908 rows, 360 people (390 per-window tracks); Hotel 6,544 rows, 390 people (433 tracks); 12.9 and 12.0 minutes (docs/DATA.md, tools/data_cards.py). Walkers move alike in both scenes (walking speed 1.46 / 1.38 m/s); Hotel has many standers (25.1% of time below 0.2 m/s, 16.3% of rows annotated with exactly zero velocity).
What it does not contain
No robot (robot columns padded with 99 on 100% of real rows), no obstacles or walls (obstacle columns padded on 100%), no goals, no intent or manoeuvre labels (our mode label is derived from the next 2 s of annotated velocity, not annotated intent), no behaviour switch, no occlusion record (tracks are what the annotators drew; identity is as annotated). 25.1% (ETH) and 35.4% (Hotel) of frames have nobody in view; ETH has 12 annotation stalls and 19 rows above 3 m/s (DATA.md integrity checks).

Simulated data: what exactly is simulated

One episode = one robot (agent 0) and n_people people in a 20 × 20 m plaza with 2 circular pillars, advanced by explicit Euler steps of dt = 0.2 s for 40 s (201 frames including t = 0). Each step, every person's acceleration is the sum of the terms below (crowd.py: CrowdSim.step); the robot gets no force, its velocity is set directly. The model form is the social force model of Helbing & Molnár (Phys. Rev. E 1995, REFERENCES [22]); every parameter value, the anticipation, the anisotropy, the obstacle steering and the regimes are our own choices, not fitted to data and not validated against real trajectories (REFERENCES row 14).

crowd.py: REGIMES (values are ours). Each person draws a fixed multiplicative jitter N(1, 0.1) clipped to [0.7, 1.3] for v0, A, B and the event rates (CrowdConfig.jitter, reset). Encoding: how every characteristic becomes a number

One row
One person, one moment t, one horizon h (features.build_rows). Rows every 0.5 s per person (row_every, D-11), only if the person was seen at every step of the history window (no NaN history, no fresh Kalman restart; f2), never for the robot.
Frame
Everything is expressed in the person's own frame: x along the heading θ, y across (+ = left), via _rot(θ). θ = filtered velocity heading above 0.2 m/s; else the displacement over the history window if above 0.05 m; else (standing) the direction to the robot, else to the nearest person (f2). Never the world x-axis. So the model sees no absolute position, no world heading, no scene layout beyond the nearest obstacle.
Not encoded
identity, absolute position and time, goals, regime, kind (one kind per scene so far; DEPLOYMENT §5 would declare it categorical once there are several), anything about people beyond the 3 nearest (except the count within 3 m).
Missing values
A missing neighbour, robot or obstacle is coded as distance 99 (FAR), offsets (99, 0), velocities and closing speed 0; the obstacle padding is not rotated into the agent frame (f2, after the f1 heading leak). A missing history step drops the row instead (f2).
Why not NaN
DEPLOYMENT §5 notes that TabPFN handles NaN natively and that 99 is a made-up value. It proposes an A/B test (Hotel → ETH and the crowd, 5 seeds, TabPFN and LightGBM) and sets the bar: adopt only if the gain exceeds the seed-to-seed spread and there is time to rerun, because switching invalidates every result so far and touches the kNN bank (scikit-learn rejects NaN), the robot_dist < 90 test in failure_modes.py, and check_shift.py. Not run: no result file for that A/B exists, so 99/0 stays and is reported as a finding.
Categorical, scaling
No categorical columns; nothing scaled or one-hot encoded (DEPLOYMENT §5, Prior Labs docs: "don't scale or one-hot encode"). Two models, one per axis (ddx, ddy).
