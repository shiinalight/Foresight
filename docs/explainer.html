How we test

On a real robot

Our baseline is close to the published one
Constant velocity at 4.8 s
Within 3–14% of the values published by Schöller et al. (RA-L 2020): ETH 1.12–1.15 m and Hotel 0.46–0.58 m (ours, depending on window selection) against 1.15 m and 0.51 m.
Why it matters
A weak baseline inflates every gain. Our first filter setting made constant velocity about 25–30% worse than published; it was fixed and every result is being rerun on the fixed features (version f4).
Sources
TabPFN
Hollmann et al., Nature 2025: tabular foundation model, in-context learning on small tables. Our TabPFN-3.5 is described in a Prior Labs technical report (2026), not peer reviewed.
Choosing example rows
Thomas et al., NeurIPS 2024 (LoCalPFN): nearest-neighbour context improves TabPFN (shown for classification; we apply it to regression by analogy).
Constant velocity is a strong baseline
Schöller et al., RA-L 2020.
Data
Pellegrini et al., ICCV 2009 (ETH, Hotel), via OpenTraj (Amirian et al., ACCV 2020).
Crowd simulation
Helbing & Molnár, Phys. Rev. E 1995 (social force). Our simulator is our own; it shares evaluation metrics with CrowdNav (Chen et al., ICRA 2019) but is not that testbed.
Ranges in planning
Lindemann et al., RA-L 2023 (conformal prediction for safe planning). Their guarantee assumes the robot doesn't change how people move, so we claim no safety guarantee.
Calibration layer
Gibbs & Candès, NeurIPS 2021 (adaptive conformal inference): long-run average coverage, not per-step.
Freezing robot
Trautman & Krause, IROS 2010. We measure freezing; we don't claim to solve it.
Baselines
van den Berg et al., ICRA 2008 (velocity obstacles; ours is a sampling variant without reciprocity); Fox et al. 1997 (dynamic window; our reactive planner is DWA-style).
Social navigation metrics
Francis et al., ACM THRI 2025 (0.5 m personal-space threshold, as in our intrusion metric).

Glossary
TabPFN
Pretrained model for tables: reads example rows, predicts a distribution for each query, no training.
Range, box
5% to 95% on both axes; holds the truth 80–90% of the time if the model is honest (81% if the two axes are independent).
Coverage
How often the truth lands in the box. Below target = overconfident, the dangerous direction.
Calibration layer
One number per look-ahead that widens or narrows boxes to keep coverage on target.
Constant velocity
Assume people keep their speed and direction. Baseline, and the reference for the target.
Intrusion
Seconds with someone within 0.5 m of the robot.
Paired crowds
Every method faces the same simulated crowds.
