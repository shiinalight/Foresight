Foresight

A robot among people predicts where each person will be in 1 and 2 seconds, as a range, using TabPFN, then picks a move that stays clear.

Where will everyone be in two seconds, and how sure are we?

Direction-dependent

A · Honest ranges in a new place

Two real scenes: on target going to Hotel (86%), short but closest going to ETH (75%). Five real scenes, each held out once: it ties a LightGBM trained on 15 times more data from four places, and beats LightGBM given the same few examples in 4 of 5. Its edge is when data is scarce.

Simulation, 3 crowds

B · Adapts by reading the scene

Helped offline and in the robot loop, in three different simulated crowds: 7 to 20 points more people inside the box than LightGBM after a behaviour change, more in denser crowds. On real pedestrians the gain is small: slightly better ranges (interval score 1.37 to 1.33), the same share of people inside. Real scenes have no sudden behaviour change to adapt to.

Not supported

C · Beats standard navigation?

No. The robot was slower than standard planners at similar safety: better ranges did not make it faster. All three learned planners, TabPFN or not, took about 26 s at the same caution setting. Same verdict in two more crowds. In the densest crowd it had about half the collisions of standard planners at similar speed (a hint, not significant).

Measured

D · Deployable

On a GPU the TabPFN step takes 71 ms (NVIDIA L4) and live TabPFN runs at 287–321 ms per plan on a MacBook (M3 Pro), inside the 0.4 s replan. Late predictions are dangerous: 0.4 s stale quadrupled crowds with a collision (7.5% to 30%).

Simulation, offline

Second form factor: cars

The same core, unchanged, on highway cars at 24 m/s: TabPFN from 1,500 examples matches LightGBM trained on 100,000 rows, with more honest ranges (86% of cars inside against 69–82%), and after the traffic suddenly turns aggressive, reading the scene puts 77% of cars inside the box against 63% for the frozen model.

Simulation, in the loop

TabPFN picks the move

A second TabPFN learned from 2,400 decisions of our planner and then drove the robot itself on 20 new crowds: same goals reached, about the same time and safety, safety checks stepped in on under 1% of moves. It shows TabPFN can be the controller, not a better one.

Real data, offline

Intent, then ranges

TabPFN used twice in a chain: it guesses each person's next move, then gives a range per move. Its ranges catch more of the real people who turn (37 people); no help in our simulator, where turns are random.

01The problem

A best guess is not enough: the robot needs to know how wrong it might be.

Honest (calibrated) = the truth lands inside as often as promised. Hardest in a new place.

02What exists today

Public material shows robots among people mostly reacting to where people are now.

Stop and go

Delivery robots: stop when something is in front; a remote human helps.

Reactive planner

ROS 2 Nav2: 0.55 m default margin around obstacles as they are now; core Nav2 does not predict people.

Self-driving predictors

Deep networks on large driving datasets: 6 possible paths, 8 s ahead.

The gap Foresight targets

Honest 1–2 s ranges per person that adapt to a new place without retraining.

Nav2 docs (2026); Starship FAQ; Ettinger et al. 2021 (Waymo Open Motion).

03The idea in one picture

Each person becomes a table row. TabPFN, pretrained to learn from tables, reads solved examples and returns a range per person in one pass, no training.

04Step by step: one real decision

One moment of a simulated run, through every stage.

STEP 1 · SCENE

A crowd crosses a plaza; the robot (purple) heads for its goal (star). We follow person P.

STEP 2 · SENSING

A Kalman filter (a standard tracker) turns noisy positions into position and velocity.

STEP 3 · ONE TABLE ROW
Look-ahead2 s
P's speed1.02 m/s
Turning rate−0.50 rad/s (right)
Where P was 1 s ago1.17 m behind
Nearest person1.78 m
Robot1.69 m, closing 0.97 m/s
To predict: offset from walking straight → ?

P becomes one row of 39 numbers, from P's point of view; six shown.

STEP 4 · EXAMPLES

TabPFN reads examples with known outcomes (dots): past crowds plus this scene's latest seconds.

STEP 5 · P'S RANGE

Out comes P's range at +1 s and +2 s.

STEP 6 · EVERYONE

One call gives everyone's box, also drawn as a map of where people may be.

STEP 7 · CHOOSE A MOVE

Candidate moves that enter a box are rejected (red); the clear one with most progress wins.

STEP 8 · REPEAT

Repeat every 0.4 s: the robot's whole path.

Final planner (frozen bank + recent scene rows, online offset), seed 6001, 7.2 s: two seconds after the crowd turned erratic. Full tables in the internal reference.

05Why TabPFN

Rival: LightGBM, a standard tree model trained beforehand.

06What we compared against

Same crowds for every robot; each baseline tuned over its own settings.

[Final robot runs: done]

20 fresh crowds that turn erratic after 5 s, judged by a rule written in advance. The TabPFN robot beat none of the four standard planners: at similar safety it was slower, for example 4.3 s slower than walk-straight with a 0.4 m margin at equal clearance. Its boxes were more honest after the change (59% of people inside against 49% for the frozen model, still short of 80%), but that did not change the driving: at the same caution setting the TabPFN and LightGBM robots all took about 26 s. A robot given people's true future paths (under its current move) kept more clearance (0.22 m against 0.09 m) but mostly did not clearly cut collisions in 20 crowds. 07How we kept it honest

Rules before results

Judging rules committed before the runs they govern.

Small tests first

Two failed tests stopped work instead of being tuned away.

Leaks found and fixed

Two leaking features removed; older results withdrawn.

Baseline close to published

Walk-straight error at 4.8 s: 1.12 / 0.46 m vs 1.15 / 0.51 m (Schöller et al. 2020).

Intervals over people

Resampling whole people or crowds, not single rows.

Every decision logged

Each chosen value has a dated row with its evidence.

08Where it breaks, and what's next

Adapting: simulation only

On real pedestrians, nothing beat walking straight.

Direction-dependent

Short going to ETH. Two real scenes, no robot in them.

No safety guarantee

Ranges buy efficiency; safety needs a worst-case floor.

Speed not proven

About 3 s per moment on an emulated laptop CPU; no robot-hardware timing.

Research licence

TabPFN-3.5 weights are non-commercial; v2 weights are Apache 2.0.

Next

A planner that uses the range's shape, not only its width; robot-hardware timing, a fallback when TabPFN is late, real robot data.
