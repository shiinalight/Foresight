Foresight

The robot's knowledge is a table of examples. It adapts to new behaviour, and to a new body, by swapping rows, with no training step.

Where will everyone be in two seconds, and how sure are we?

Simulation, in the loop

1 · Adapts when behaviour changes

After the crowd turns erratic, reading the scene's last seconds brought the ranges closer to their promise: 59% of people inside the 2 s box against 41% for LightGBM (+18 points), 10 of those points from the scene alone. Ahead of LightGBM in all three crowds tried. Still short of the 81% target.

Simulation, offline

2 · Same core, another body: cars

The same core on simulated highway traffic: TabPFN on 1,500 rows matches a LightGBM trained on 67 times more, with more honest ranges (86% of cars inside against 69–82%). When traffic turns aggressive, reading the scene adds 14 points of coverage (77% vs 63%).

Real data, offline

3 · Learns more per example

On five real pedestrian scenes, each held out once, TabPFN beats LightGBM on the same 1,500 rows and ties a LightGBM trained on about 15 times more.

Simulation, in the loop

TabPFN at every learned step

A second TabPFN learned from 2,400 decisions of our planner, then drove the robot itself on 20 new crowds. On average it drove like the planner it learned from: same goals reached, about the same time; safety checks stepped in on under 1% of moves. It shows TabPFN can be the controller, not a better one.

Narrowed

Honest ranges in a scene never seen

Holds on two scenes, not five. Going Hotel → ETH, TabPFN's box held 75% of people against 58% for LightGBM. On five scenes it ties LightGBM trained on about 15 times more rows.

Not supported

Beats standard navigation?

No. Our robot was slower than standard planners: 4.3 s slower than walk-straight at equal clearance, 3.0 s at equal collision rate. Better ranges did not change how this planner drives; its caution setting did. Same verdict in three crowds.

Measured

Speed, measured

71 ms per replan on an NVIDIA L4, which fits the 0.4 s replan period; 287–321 ms per plan on a MacBook (M3 Pro). 10 Hz is not claimed: it needs a background rebuild, designed, not built. Late predictions are dangerous: 0.4 s stale quadrupled crowds with a collision (7.5% to 30%).

Real data, offline

Intent, then ranges

TabPFN used twice in a chain: a classifier labels each person's likely manoeuvre, then a range per manoeuvre. Better-calibrated ranges for the real people who turn (37 people, 46% → 63% inside); worse in our simulator.

01The problem

A best guess is not enough: the robot needs to know how wrong it might be.

Honest (calibrated) = the truth lands inside as often as promised. Hardest right after behaviour changes, when examples are few.

02What exists today

Public material shows robots among people mostly reacting to where people are now.

Stop and go

Delivery robots: stop when something is in front; a remote human helps.

Reactive planner

ROS 2 Nav2: 0.55 m default margin around obstacles as they are now; core Nav2 does not predict people.

Self-driving predictors

Deep networks on large driving datasets: 6 possible paths, 8 s ahead.

The gap Foresight targets

1–2 s ranges per person that adapt to new behaviour, and to a new body, by swapping example rows, with no training step.

Nav2 docs (2026); Starship FAQ; Ettinger et al. 2021 (Waymo Open Motion).

03The idea in one picture

Each person becomes a table row. TabPFN, pretrained to learn from tables, reads solved examples and returns a range per person in one pass, no training.

One moment of a simulated run, through every stage.

A crowd crosses a plaza; the robot (purple) heads for its goal (star). We follow person P.

STEP 2 · SENSING

STEP 4 · EXAMPLES

TabPFN reads examples with known outcomes (dots): past crowds plus this scene's latest seconds.

STEP 5 · P'S RANGE

Out comes P's range at +1 s and +2 s.

STEP 6 · EVERYONE

One call gives everyone's box, also drawn as a map of where people may be.

STEP 7 · CHOOSE A MOVE

Candidate moves that enter a box are rejected (red); the clear one with most progress wins.

STEP 8 · REPEAT

Repeat every 0.4 s: the robot's whole path.

05Why TabPFN

Rival: LightGBM, a standard tree model trained beforehand.

06Same core, another body: cars

The same core, with a cars adapter, on simulated highway traffic. TabPFN on 1,500 rows matches a LightGBM trained on 67 times more (100,440 rows), with more honest ranges: 86% of cars inside the box against 69–82%. When traffic turns aggressive, reading the scene adds 14 points of coverage (77% vs 63%).

Each body needs its own examples: pedestrian examples used on cars give boxes about 50 m wide. The code carries over; the example table is swapped.

07Five real pedestrian scenes

ETH, Hotel, Zara1, Zara2 and Univ, each held out once and predicted from the other four. TabPFN on 1,500 rows beats LightGBM on the same rows (interval score −0.130 [−0.154, −0.107]) and ties LightGBM on all rows of the other four scenes, about 15 times more (−0.008 [−0.023, +0.007]).

08What we compared against

Same crowds for every robot; each baseline tuned over its own settings.

Final robot runs

20 fresh crowds that turn erratic after 5 s, judged by a rule written in advance. The TabPFN robot beat none of the four standard planners: it was slower, for example 4.3 s slower than walk-straight with a 0.4 m margin at equal clearance, 3.0 s at equal collision rate. Its boxes came closer to their promise after the change (59% of people inside against 49% for the frozen model and 41% for LightGBM, still short of the 81% target), but that did not change the driving: at the same caution setting the TabPFN and LightGBM robots all took about 26 s. A robot given people's true future paths (under its current move) kept more clearance (0.22 m against 0.09 m) but mostly did not clearly cut collisions in 20 crowds. 09How we kept it honest

Rules before results

Judging rules committed before the runs they govern.

Small tests first

Two failed tests stopped work instead of being tuned away.

Leaks found and fixed

Two leaking features removed; older results withdrawn.

Baseline close to published

Walk-straight error at 4.8 s: 1.12 / 0.46 m vs 1.15 / 0.51 m (Schöller et al. 2020).

Intervals over people

Resampling whole people or crowds, not single rows.

Every decision logged

Each chosen value has a dated row with its evidence.

10Where it breaks, and what's next

Slower than standard planners

Better ranges did not change how this box-veto planner drives; its caution setting did.

Adapting: small on real pedestrians

Real scenes have no sudden switch; the gain is small (interval score −0.045 [−0.080, −0.009]), same coverage.

Simulated loop and cars

Forecasts tested on five real scenes, each held out once, with no robot in them. The robot loop and the cars are simulated; cars offline only.

Each body needs its own examples

The code carries over; the example table does not.

No safety guarantee

Ranges buy efficiency; safety needs a worst-case floor.

Speed: measured on a GPU

71 ms per replan on an NVIDIA L4, inside the 0.4 s replan period. 10 Hz is not claimed: it needs a background rebuild, designed, not built. No robot-hardware timing.

Feature engineering not adopted

Hand-made features tied the raw row; on their own they were worse.

Research licence

TabPFN-3.5 weights are non-commercial; v2 weights are Apache 2.0.

Next

A planner that uses the range's shape, not only its width; the background rebuild for 10 Hz; a fallback when TabPFN is late; a closed loop for cars; real robot data.
